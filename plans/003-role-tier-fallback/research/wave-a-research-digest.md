## router-fallback
Researched LiteLLM Router, OpenRouter, Portkey, and general distributed-systems literature (AWS backoff/jitter, Google SRE retry budgets, classic circuit-breaker pattern, adaptive hedging) for concrete fallback/failover mechanisms and their exact trigger conditions, TTLs, and thresholds. The core pattern across all gateways is the same three-layer stack: (1) per-error-type retry with capped attempts + exponential backoff + full jitter, escalating to (2) a priority-ordered fallback chain where non-retryable errors (auth/content-policy/context-overflow) skip straight to a dedicated fallback list, protected by (3) a per-deployment circuit breaker (strike counter -> TTL cooldown -> single-probe half-open recovery) plus a run-level retry budget (~10-15% of total traffic) that caps aggregate retry volume regardless of per-lane state, with a graceful-degradation bypass when every option is simultaneously cooled down. Hedged requests are a separate, latency-driven mechanism (fire a parallel backup once the primary exceeds its own learned p90) that is explicitly expensive for token-billed LLM calls and self-throttles via a token-bucket budget. Every mechanism maps cleanly onto unimatrix's bash file-bus as flag files (cooldown open/closed), JSONL strike ledgers (pruned-by-window counters), and an flock-gated single-probe half-open recovery — with a hard recommendation to skip hedging by default given the project's zero-daemon, log-every-spend constraints, and to keep transport-level health (this research) strictly separate from the project's existing output-quality verification/judge-role concern."
KEY POINTS:
- LiteLLM Router cooldown: allowed_fails default=3 failures/minute (also trips on >50% failure rate in current minute) trips a per-deployment cooldown_time default=5s; deployment identity = hash of litellm_params (so two configs of the same model are tracked independently) — maps directly to per-lane, not per-model, health tracking.
- LiteLLM error classes are NOT treated uniformly: 429 triggers immediate cooldown; 401/404/408 are non-retryable (skip straight past retries to fallback/cooldown, no wasted attempts); RateLimitError gets exponential backoff, generic errors retry immediately; ContextWindowExceededError and ContentPolicyViolationError each route through their OWN separate fallback list (context_window_fallbacks, content_policy_fallbacks) distinct from the generic fallbacks list — i.e. fallback target depends on failure TYPE, not just 'it failed'.
- LiteLLM 'weighted failover' (enable_weighted_failover): on failure, re-pick a different deployment WITHIN the same priority tier/order level first (consuming that tier's retry budget), and only escalate to the next order tier once the whole tier is exhausted; exclusions accumulate across hops; capped by max_fallbacks default=5. This is priority-chain + intra-tier load-balancing combined, not either/or.
- Portkey circuit breaker states are just CLOSED/OPEN (no half-open in their impl) — opens on failure_threshold (raw count) OR failure_threshold_percentage (rate, only evaluated after a minimum_requests sample size is reached); failure_status_codes defaults to 500+; cooldown_interval is specified in milliseconds with a documented MINIMUM of 30 seconds; critically, when ALL targets in a routing group are OPEN, Portkey bypasses the breaker and makes every target available again rather than hard-failing — graceful degradation over total outage.
- Classic circuit-breaker pattern (Azure Architecture Center / textbook) has three states: closed (normal) -> open (fails fast, no dispatch attempted at all, avoiding a full timeout wait) -> half-open (after cooldown, allow exactly 1..k probe requests; success closes the circuit, failure re-opens it, typically with backed-off cooldown). Half-open's single-probe gate is the specific mechanism that prevents a thundering herd of everyone retrying the instant a TTL expires.
- Hysteresis / anti-flapping: don't switch on any single failure or any single 'looks a bit better' signal — require a rate/count threshold to trip (allowed_fails, failure_threshold_percentage) AND a margin before switching back (switching margin δ: only prefer a challenger if Score(challenger) > Score(current)+δ, plus a minimum cooldown/dwell period) — trips and un-trips must not use the same hair-trigger, or the system flaps.
- OpenRouter default routing (no explicit order/sort): (1) exclude providers with 'significant outages in the last 30 seconds' (a 30s rolling health window), (2) among survivors, weight selection by inverse-square of price — e.g. $1/M vs $3/M provider gets 9x more traffic (1/3²) — a strongly cost-skewed load balance, not round-robin, (3) remaining stable providers become the ordered fallback list. allow_fallbacks=true is the default; setting sort to price/throughput/latency disables the load-balancing weighting entirely in favor of a strict ordering.
- Retry budgets (Google SRE, cross-validated by industry blogs): cap total retries as a PERCENTAGE OF TOTAL TRAFFIC, not an absolute per-request count — SRE book example uses a ~10% retry-ratio budget (tracked as retries/total-requests over a rolling window); once the ratio is exceeded, further retries are simply refused (request fails fast) rather than queued or delayed. This is what actually prevents a partial outage (e.g. real load at 3x due to naive per-request retries) from cascading — SRE's worked example shows capped retries hold total load to ~1.1x baseline vs ~3x uncapped. Other sources (Portkey/tianpan.co) independently converge on a similar 5-15% global retry-budget range.
- Exponential backoff MUST include full jitter to be effective: sleep(min(cap, base * 2^attempt) * random(0.5, 1.0)) is the concrete formula cited (AWS Architecture Blog canonical form, ~8 years of production use across AWS SDKs). Backoff alone reduces volume; jitter is what decorrelates synchronized clients (the 'thundering herd' of N instances all retrying in lockstep after a shared blip) — the two are complementary, not substitutes, and most guidance also caps retry ATTEMPTS low (2-3) before falling back rather than retrying-in-place many times.
- Hedged requests (tail-latency mitigation, distinct from failure-triggered fallback): fire a second, parallel request to a backup when the primary exceeds a LEARNED percentile of its own latency distribution (e.g., p90, computed via a sketch like DDSketch with ~30s rotating windows merged for a 30-60s effective observation window) — NOT a fixed timeout. Concrete numbers from one production write-up: refill_rate = estimatedRPS * budgetPercent/100 with budgetPercent default 10%, so hedge extra-load is hard-capped via a token bucket (e.g., 1000 RPS * 10% = 100 tokens, drains in ~1s under a full outage) so that hedging automatically throttles itself to near-zero exactly when it would otherwise amplify an outage. BigTable's canonical hedging benchmark: a 10ms hedge-delay cut p99.9 from 1800ms to 74ms for only a 2% increase in total request volume — hedging is cheap when it only fires on the genuine slow tail.
- Hedging has an LLM-specific cost trap absent in classic RPC hedging: an LLM call costs real tokens/GPU-seconds, so a fired-but-discarded hedge is not free the way a redundant read is — sources explicitly flag 'if each call costs a $0.02 token bill, hedging means paying twice for ~5% of requests' and recommend propagating a cancel to the losing in-flight request the instant the winner returns, plus measuring hedge trigger off first-body-byte (TTFT) rather than first-header-byte for streaming responses.
- Health-check philosophy split: ACTIVE health checks (periodic synthetic probes against each backend) vs PASSIVE health checks (infer health purely from real traffic's own error/latency signal, e.g. requests/tokens/failures-per-minute crossing a threshold). Passive is repeatedly called out as cheaper and more representative of actual conditions, and is the default LiteLLM/most-gateway approach; active probing is an add-on for catching outages between real requests, not the primary mechanism.
- Non-retryable vs retryable is a first-class distinction everywhere: auth failures (401), not-found (404), content-policy violations, and context-window-exceeded should NEVER consume retry budget or a generic strike — they should route immediately to a dedicated fallback (or fail the whole chain) because retrying an auth-dead credential or an inherently-too-long prompt cannot succeed and only wastes the retry/cooldown ceremony meant for transient (429/5xx/timeout) failures.
- 'Deployment' identity granularity matters for cooldown correctness: LiteLLM cooldowns are keyed to a deterministic hash of the full deployment config (not just the model name string), so two lane instances that share a model but differ in credentials/region/etc are tracked and cooled down independently — directly relevant if unimatrix ever runs two configs of the same underlying model/binary as separate lanes.
RECOMMENDATIONS:
- Per-lane cooldown flag file: `.bus/limits/<lane>.cooldown` — `touch` on trip, presence + `stat -c%Y` age vs a TTL is the 'open' check. Anchor the TTL between LiteLLM's 5s (aggressive, many small deployments) and Portkey's 30s-minimum (fewer, costlier lanes): given unimatrix has only 6 lanes and each spawn is relatively expensive (a full CLI invocation), default toward the Portkey end — 30-60s — not LiteLLM's 5s.
- Strike counter as JSONL (matches existing 'one JSONL record = one write(2) call' convention): `.bus/limits/<lane>-strikes.jsonl`, one line per failure with a timestamp; before tripping cooldown, `jq`/awk-prune entries older than a 60s window and count what remains; trip cooldown when count >= allowed_fails (start at 3, LiteLLM's default, and tune from real run-evidence).
- Classify failures into two ceremonies, not one: (a) transient (429/5xx/timeout/connection-refused) -> strike counter + TTL cooldown as above; (b) terminal (auth/credential dead, quota permanently exhausted, content-policy/refusal) -> a separate stronger flag file with NO TTL (e.g. `.bus/limits/<lane>.dead`) that requires the next `/swarm` invocation or explicit operator clear to remove — never auto-expires, never consumes the transient strike budget. This maps LiteLLM's non-retryable-error and context/content-policy-specific-fallback-list ideas onto the bus.
- Half-open probe via flock to avoid a thundering herd the instant a cooldown TTL expires: after TTL, the first card that reaches the lane takes an `flock -n` on `.bus/limits/<lane>.probe-lock`; only that single card actually dispatches to the lane while cooldown is still nominally 'open' for everyone else (they fall straight to the next EXEC_CHAIN lane without waiting). Probe success deletes the cooldown flag (close); probe failure re-touches it with a backed-off TTL (double it, capped) — this is the concrete bash equivalent of the classic closed/open/half-open circuit breaker and directly prevents every queued card from re-hammering a lane the second it's technically eligible again.
- Global retry budget ledger: one line appended to `.bus/limits/global-retries.jsonl` per retry issued across the whole run; before issuing a retry, compute (retries in ledger) / (total card dispatches so far) and refuse further retries (fail the card straight to the next EXEC_CHAIN lane, no sleep-and-retry) once that ratio exceeds ~10-15%, mirroring Google SRE's retry-budget pattern. This is a run-level safety valve on top of per-lane cooldowns — protects against a run where every lane is individually 'fine' but the aggregate retry volume is still exploding.
- Sleep formula for any in-place retry (rare — most failures should escalate along EXEC_CHAIN rather than retry the same lane): `sleep_s=$(awk -v b=1 -v c=30 -v a=$attempt 'BEGIN{d=b*(2^a); if(d>c)d=c; srand(); print d*(0.5+rand()*0.5)}')` — full jitter, base=1s, cap=30s, matching the AWS canonical formula. Cap attempts at 2-3 before escalating to the next lane in EXEC_CHAIN, not more.
- Priority-ordered EXEC_CHAIN already matches the 'priority-based failover' pattern from every source (order=1,2,3... first-healthy-wins) — keep it, but add the LiteLLM 'weighted failover' refinement only if/when a role gets more than one same-tier lane option: retry within tier (e.g., multiple claude sub-tiers) before falling to the next tier, capped at a small max_fallbacks (e.g. 5) to bound total chain length per card.
- Do NOT build hedged/duplicate-dispatch requests as a default mechanism — it doubles LLM spend on the exact traffic it fires on, requires live p90 latency tracking (DDSketch-style) that a daemon-free bash file-bus has no cheap way to maintain, and the project's own conventions (zero daemon, log every spend) cut against silently duplicating billed calls. If ever added, gate it behind a static per-lane timeout pulled from docs/versions.md (not a learned percentile), hard-cap concurrent hedges to 1 via a semaphore lockfile, and log the hedge explicitly in the run-evidence ledger as its own line item (it is real spend, and 'no silent spend' is already a hard project rule).
- Health signal should stay passive, matching both the gateway literature's preference and the project's existing 'judge != executor' rule: infer a lane's health from real card outcomes already flowing through the bus (strike counter above), never from a lane self-reporting its own status — a lane claiming health is exactly as untrustworthy as a lane claiming a correct answer (the project's documented 'grok false-done pattern' is the same failure class one level up: self-attestation without independent check). Explicitly do not conflate transport-level health (this research) with output-quality verification (VERIFY_MAP/judge roles) — a lane can be transport-healthy (200 OK, fast) and still produce a false-done/fabricated card result; only the judge role catches that, no cooldown/strike mechanism here will.
- Track cooldown/strike state per lane INSTANCE (e.g., per swarm.conf lane entry / per credential), not per underlying model name, in case unimatrix ever runs two configs of the same binary (e.g. two Grok keys, or grok vs GLM sharing the claude-child-env-swap mechanism) as distinct lanes — mirrors LiteLLM's hash-of-full-deployment-config identity so one bad credential doesn't cool down a sibling lane that happens to share a model name.

## cascade-routing
Researched how real systems operationalize cheapest-capable LLM routing, from the founding FrugalGPT cascade paper through 2026 successors. Two competing designs exist: cascades (try cheap, escalate on a post-hoc confidence/quality signal — FrugalGPT, Cluster-Route-Escalate, UCCI) and upfront/pre-generation routers (classify the query first, pick one model, never run a discarded cheap pass — RouteLLM, IRT-Router, BEST-Route). The most direct 2026 answer to 'which is better' (a decision-theoretic cascade characterization paper) found pre-generation routing beats cascading on 4 of 5 benchmarks specifically because cascading always pays the structural cost of the cheap model's run, and that 2-stage cascades capture almost all cascade value anyway — deeper chains barely help. Confidence signals used for escalation (token entropy/margin, self-verification, hidden-state probes, post-hoc output classifiers) are consistently reported as unreliable raw and need calibration (e.g. isotonic regression in UCCI) against real outcome data before being trusted as a threshold. Reported cost savings run from a conservative 31% (deployed production NER) to headline 85-98% (narrow academic benchmarks), with 40-85% at ~95% quality retention being the most commonly repeated production-grade range. Static heuristic routing (keyword/length rules) is explicitly endorsed as sufficient until traffic/data volume justifies a trained classifier. Anthropic-specific cascade guidance found online was low-quality SEO content with fabricated model names/pricing and should not be trusted as fact.</summary>

KEY POINTS:
- FrugalGPT (Chen/Zaharia/Zou, Stanford, 2023, arxiv 2305.05176) — the founding LLM-cascade paper. Cascade = query cheapest model first; a trained DistilBERT scoring function g(q,a) outputs a reliability score in [0,1]; if score > per-stage threshold, accept and stop, else escalate to the next-costlier model. Model order and per-stage thresholds are set by grid search on a validation set, not learned end-to-end. Result: matches GPT-4 accuracy at up to 98% lower cost, or beats GPT-4 accuracy by 4% at equal cost. This is the canonical 'try-cheap-then-escalate-on-failure' design.
- RouteLLM (LMSYS/Ong et al., 2024, arxiv 2406.18665) is UPFRONT (pre-generation) routing, not a cascade — it never runs the cheap model to decide. Trains a binary strong/weak-model router on Chatbot Arena human-preference data plus augmentation (LLM-judge labels, benchmark golden-labels), via four interchangeable architectures: similarity-weighted Elo ranking, matrix factorization (a learned prompt-model scoring function), a BERT classifier, and a causal-LLM classifier. Reported cost reduction at 95%-of-GPT-4 quality: ~85% on MT-Bench, ~45% on MMLU, ~35% on GSM8K — savings vary a lot by benchmark, showing routing gains are task-distribution dependent and don't generalize uniformly.
- The most directly relevant 2026 paper to the upfront-vs-cascade question is 'Is Escalation Worth It? A Decision-Theoretic Characterization of LLM Cascades' (arxiv 2605.06350). It formalizes cascades as constrained optimization (min cost s.t. quality floor / max quality s.t. budget) solved via Lagrangian duality, with escalation triggered by a single threshold on a white-box confidence signal (mean token negentropy performed best; min-token-prob, margin, and sequence-probability were also tested). Empirically across MATH/MMLU/TriviaQA/SimpleQA/LiveCodeBench and 8 models: (a) pairwise (2-model) cascades captured almost all the value — adding more stages beat the best pairwise choice by only 0.014 normalized gain; (b) a lightweight EMBEDDING-BASED PRE-GENERATION router beat the best cascade policy outright on 4 of 5 datasets, because cascading always pays the structural cost of running the cheap model first, even on queries where it will be discarded; (c) up to 79.5% cost reduction at 90% of ceiling quality vs. always-frontier.
- Confidence/success signals used for escalation in practice are heterogeneous and none are fully trustworthy raw: token-level log-prob signals (entropy, margin, min-prob, sequence-prob) are commonly used but repeatedly described as miscalibrated and prompt-wording-sensitive (survey arxiv 2603.04445; UCCI arxiv 2605.18796). Fixes in the literature: UCCI applies isotonic regression to calibrate a token-margin uncertainty score into an error probability (cut ECE from 0.12 to 0.03) before thresholding, achieving 31% cost reduction at fixed quality on a production NER task, beating entropy-thresholding, conformal prediction, and FrugalGPT-style routing at the same operating point. Other signal families: hidden-state probes trained on internal activations (found most reliable by Chuang et al. per the survey), self-verification via few-shot in-context prompting (AutoMix), and lightweight fine-tuning with special confidence tokens (Self-REF). Post-hoc output classifiers are also used: 'Cluster, Route, Escalate' (arxiv 2606.27457) trains a ModernBERT classifier on the executor's OUTPUT text to decide accept/escalate, needing no extra annotation beyond correctness labels — but on one benchmark (TeleQnA) ~51% of its escalations were false positives, i.e. post-hoc quality classifiers themselves carry a real error/waste rate.
- Static heuristic tiers vs trained routers: the consensus across production-routing writeups (LiteLLM docs, n8n, markaicode, TrueFoundry) is that static rules (token count, keyword/regex for code or 'step by step' reasoning markers, simple-query phrases like 'what is') are near-zero-latency and 'capture 80% of the value with a few dozen lines of code' for well-defined, predictable workloads. Trained classifiers (small BERT-class models on your own labeled workload) outperform heuristics but need enough production volume/data to be worth the added latency and infra; they become worthwhile mainly as task diversity and traffic scale up. This matches unimatrix's own low-QPS, single-operator profile — favors static heuristic first.
- Task-difficulty-aware routing has a distinct academic line built on psychometrics: IRT-Router (ACL 2025, arxiv 2506.01048) applies Item Response Theory to jointly model latent 'query difficulty' and 'model ability' parameters from embeddings, predicting P(correct) per (query, model) pair and combining it with cost in the routing score — explicitly interpretable (you can read off ability/difficulty numbers) versus opaque classifier scores. UniRoute (2026) extends routing to models unseen at training/deployment time, relevant if unimatrix adds lanes later.
- Repeatedly reported failure mode across sources: the router/cascade mis-classifies a hard query as easy, and the cheap model returns a confidently-wrong answer that passes the accept threshold — 'a drifting verifier can silently escalate everything' or nothing, and this needs continuous production monitoring, not a one-time calibration. This is the exact risk in a '>=90% confident' cheapest-lane policy: the confidence estimate itself must be monitored/recalibrated against real outcomes over time, not trusted as a static number.
- Reported cost-savings ranges cluster tightly across independent sources: FrugalGPT up to 98% (best case, narrow eval), RouteLLM 85%/45%/35% (benchmark-dependent), decision-theoretic cascade paper 79.5% at 90% ceiling quality, MixLLM (surveyed) 97.25% of GPT-4 quality at 24.18% of cost, R2-Reasoner 84.46% cost savings on reasoning tasks, UCCI 31% on a real production NER pipeline (a much more conservative, deployed number), and the general industry-blog consensus figure is 40-85% cost reduction while retaining ~95%+ quality, with well-calibrated cascades escalating roughly 15-35% of traffic to the frontier tier in practice. Deployed/production numbers (UCCI 31%, Cluster-Route-Escalate ~2-3pp accuracy recovery with ~51% false-positive escalation on one dataset) are markedly less dramatic than paper headline numbers (85-98%) — treat the biggest published percentages as best-case/narrow-benchmark, not typical production experience.
- Production/commercial routers add a layer papers don't cover: prompt adaptation alongside model selection. Not Diamond's router not only predicts the best downstream LLM per query but also rewrites the prompt to suit the chosen model, reporting 5-60% accuracy improvements from customers (one case: 10% quality up with 10% cost/latency down simultaneously). vLLM Semantic Router (CNCF/Red Hat, v0.1 'Iris', Jan 2026) bundles model selection with safety filtering (jailbreak/PII), semantic caching, and hallucination detection into one open-source 'mixture-of-models' layer, and is adding RL-driven selection tuned to user preference signals — i.e. the frontier is moving toward routers with multiple joint objectives (cost, safety, cache-hit, hallucination risk), not cost/quality alone.
- Anthropic-specific model-cascade guidance found was entirely from third-party SEO/blog aggregators (sitepoint, mindstudio, datrick, ayautomate, knightli, aiforanything), not Anthropic's own docs — flag as unverified, and several cited model names/prices ('Opus 4.8', 'Sonnet 4.6', 'Haiku 4.5') do not match any confirmed real Anthropic release and read as likely AI-generated content-farm fabrication; do not treat these numbers as fact. The one structurally useful (source-agnostic) idea repeated there: treat each pipeline STAGE (plan / execute / review) as its own separate routing decision rather than picking one model for the whole task — claimed 30-50% cost reduction at the workflow level, which is architecturally identical to what unimatrix already does via PLAN/EXEC_CHAIN/REVIEW being separate roles in swarm.conf.
RECOMMENDATIONS:
- Route upfront (pre-generation) by default, not cascade-by-default. The strongest empirical result found (decision-theoretic cascade paper) is that a cheap embedding/heuristic classifier deciding the lane BEFORE any lane runs beats a run-cheap-then-escalate cascade on 4/5 tasks, because cascading always pays the cost of the cheap run even when discarded. For unimatrix cards, classify card metadata already available for free (file count touched, spec cross-refs, presence of 'refactor'/'design'/'ambiguous' keywords, prior lane failure history for that card type) to pick the exec lane, rather than always running haiku first and judging after.
- Keep escalate-on-failure as the FALLBACK path, not the primary mechanism — this satisfies unimatrix's own 'every role and lane must have a fallback' requirement while matching the research: use the pre-route heuristic for the common case, and let the existing REVIEW lane (already a different lane than EXEC, satisfying judge != executor) trigger escalation only when its verdict is a genuine failure, not a routine quality re-check. Cascade papers agree 2-stage (cheap -> one escalation target) captures nearly all the benefit; a 3-rung fixed haiku->sonnet->opus chain for every card type is not supported by the evidence — pick the escalation target per card type/failure mode instead of a fixed universal ladder.
- Do not trust a model's raw self-reported confidence or codex/gemini/grok CLI exit signals as the >=90% bar directly. Every paper that tried raw confidence (entropy, self-report) found it miscalibrated and prompt-sensitive. The unimatrix-native substitute for UCCI's isotonic calibration is unimatrix's own run-evidence/speedwars ledger: calibrate each lane's real historical success rate per card-type from logged runs, and gate lane selection on that empirical number, not on the worker's own claimed confidence.
- Stay static-heuristic-first in swarm.conf, per the ladder in this codebase's own rules (zero-MCP, zero-daemon, bash-only) and per the research consensus that rule-based routing captures ~80% of the value at unimatrix's likely (low) card volume. Do not build a trained classifier/embedding router unless/until card volume and logged outcome data are large enough to make one pay off — that threshold has not been reached yet for a single-operator tool.
- When designing reviewer/verifier fallback chains for the proposed Kimi/Codex higher-tier roles, make the fallback a genuinely different model family from the primary (not just a bigger tier of the same lane) — this avoids correlated blind spots and matches both the judge != executor rule already in CLAUDE.md and the repeated 'a drifting verifier can silently escalate everything' failure mode found in the research.
- Log every escalation/no-escalation decision (which lane was picked, confidence/heuristic score, outcome) into the run-evidence ledger unimatrix already requires — this turns the mandatory no-silent-spend logging into free calibration data for periodically re-tuning the >=90% threshold, closing the loop the papers call out as necessary (continuous monitoring, not one-time calibration).

## orchestration-hierarchy
Research converges on a consistent hierarchy: planning/decomposition and adjudication (judging, verification, final review) are the functions mature systems keep on the strongest/most expensive model, while bulk execution of well-scoped subtasks is safely and profitably delegated to cheaper models — provided the plan handed down is detailed enough to collapse ambiguity. Anthropic's own multi-agent research system (orchestrator-worker: Opus 4 lead + Sonnet 4 subagents, 90.2% quality gain) and its 2026 Managed Agents docs both formalize this as "coordinator decomposes, workers execute, coordinator synthesizes" with an explicit warning against "problem-centric" splits (by role like planning/testing/review) in favor of "context-centric" splits (by independent data/subtask). Cursor's swarm-economics case study gives hard numbers: an Opus-planner + cheap-worker split cost $1,339 vs $10,565 for an all-frontier run, at equal final quality — because planners consume only 10-31% of tokens but decide the few genuinely hard moments (decomposition, design trade-offs), after which cheap models just execute the instructions. On the judge≠executor question, the literature is unambiguous and multiplies unimatrix's existing rule: LLM-as-judge systems reliably self-prefer and family-prefer their own outputs (GPT-5.2/Gemini-3.1 Pro show 75-84% self-family win rates; smarter judges are not fairer, per June-2026 findings — capability does not fix this bias, only architectural separation does), and "Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet" (ICLR'24) plus the generator-verifier asymmetry literature both support keeping verification structurally independent from generation. Coordination is not free: multi-agent overhead is real and measured (3-10x token cost vs single-agent, latency compounding at every handoff), so mature guidance (Anthropic's own "when to use multi-agent" post) says default to single-agent and only fan out for context isolation, true parallelization, or tool/domain specialization — never fan out "by role" (planner/reviewer/tester) since that's exactly the split unimatrix already uses successfully via the file-bus, which sidesteps the "telephone game" context-loss failure mode by handing full artifacts rather than summaries.
KEY POINTS:
- Anthropic's multi-agent research system (engineering.anthropic.com) is orchestrator-worker: Opus 4 lead agent plans and saves its plan to memory BEFORE spawning subagents; Sonnet 4 subagents execute focused search/analysis in parallel and return condensed findings; a separate citation agent handles attribution — three distinct role/model tiers, not one model doing everything.
- That system measured a 90.2% quality improvement over single-agent Opus 4, but costs ~15x a normal chat turn in tokens — the architecture is explicitly reserved for high-value tasks where the quality delta justifies the token spend, not applied by default.
- Cursor's agent-swarm case study (cursor.com/blog/agent-swarm-model-economics) is the strongest quantified evidence for tiering: Opus-4.8-planner + Composer-2.5-worker cost $1,339 total vs $10,565 for GPT-5.5 doing both roles, with comparable final quality (73-85% test pass in 4h, all reaching 100% eventually) — workers consumed 69-90% of tokens but only ~1/3 of cost because planner tokens are far more expensive per-token; the paper's framing: 'few moments in a large task genuinely require frontier intelligence — the original decomposition, the design decisions, certain trade-offs' and once that ambiguity is collapsed into explicit instructions, cheap models just have to follow them.
- LLM-as-judge self-bias and family-bias are empirically established and NOT fixed by using a smarter judge: 'Play Favorites' (arXiv 2508.06709, 5000+ prompt-completion pairs, 9 LLM judges) found GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet self-inflate scores, and models show family-bias — favoring same-family outputs even when not their own; a June-2026 finding (techtimes.com summarizing new research) states explicitly that making a model smarter does not make it a fairer judge and may make it MORE biased, because judge and judged share training data and stylistic priors — this directly validates unimatrix's judge≠executor rule as a structural (not capability-based) requirement.
- 'Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet' (Huang et al., ICLR'24, arXiv 2310.01798) found intrinsic self-correction without external feedback often does not improve and can degrade performance — reinforcing that verification must come from an external agent/lane, not the executor re-checking its own work.
- Generator-verifier asymmetry research: verifying is traditionally cheaper/easier than generating (maze-path analogy; unit-test verification in code/math), which is the theoretical basis for 'weak-to-strong' supervision — a cheaper/independent verifier can meaningfully grade a stronger generator's work. Caveat found: for frontier coding agents specifically, some 2026 sources note this asymmetry is narrowing as generators get more sophisticated, so verifier capability requirements are rising too — a cheap verifier lane may not stay sufficient forever.
- Anthropic's own 'when to use multi-agent systems (and when not to)' post (claude.com/blog) recommends single-agent as the DEFAULT, and multi-agent only for three cases: context protection (isolating high-volume irrelevant-to-caller context), true parallelization of independent work, and tool/domain specialization that would otherwise cause 'tool set confusion.' It explicitly WARNS against 'problem-centric decomposition' — splitting by work TYPE like planning/testing/review across agents — in favor of 'context-centric' splits by independent subtask/data, because problem-centric splits cause coordination overhead and information loss (the 'telephone game') at every handoff.
- Anthropic's 2026 Managed Agents API formalizes coordinator/worker as a first-class primitive: a coordinator can delegate to up to 20 roster agents (or spawn copies of itself), each with its own model/tools/system prompt, but delegation is capped at ONE level deep (an agent with its own multiagent roster cannot be referenced by another coordinator) — i.e., Anthropic's own product deliberately disallows deep recursive hierarchies, favoring a flat coordinator-then-workers shape over arbitrary tree depth.
- Measured coordination overhead is real and non-trivial: a 3-agent pipeline can consume ~3x the tokens of an equivalent single-agent approach (29,000 vs 10,000 tokens in one cited example), and a 4-agent pipeline can accumulate ~950ms of pure coordination/handoff latency versus ~500ms of actual processing — multi-agent should be justified by a specific bottleneck, not applied reflexively.
- LangGraph's supervisor pattern and the broader orchestrator-worker literature (buildingeffectiveagents.com, LangChain's plan-and-execute post) describe the same planner/executor split found elsewhere, adding the 'planning failure propagation' risk: if the planner produces a weak plan, the whole downstream workflow suffers regardless of executor quality — this is the argument for spending disproportionately on plan quality even when execution is cheap.
- CrewAI/AutoGen production post-mortems (2026) report the same failure classes regardless of framework: unbounded agent loops silently burn 5-10x token budget (fix: cap max_iter/turns per agent explicitly), agents duplicate side effects or retry destructively on partial failure, and handoff state ('what one agent learned vs what it passed to the next') remains the single weakest link across all frameworks — directly relevant to unimatrix's file-bus handoff files, which should carry full artifacts, not summaries, to avoid this exact class of loss.
- OpenAI's Swarm (deprecated March 2025, superseded by the Agents SDK) reduced multi-agent coordination to two primitives — Agent (instructions+tools) and Handoff (a function returning another Agent, transferring control) — and this handoff-as-control-transfer model became the de facto industry vocabulary; its successor adds guardrails, tracing, and state management on the same core model rather than changing the underlying pattern, suggesting the primitive itself (not the scaffolding) is what's durable.
- Resilience/fallback literature converges on a layered pattern directly applicable to unimatrix's VERIFY_MAP and lane-fallback requirement: (1) retry-with-backoff for transient errors, (2) an ordered model/provider fallback CHAIN (not a single backup) for outages, (3) error classification to route tool errors differently from model errors, (4) checkpoint/state recovery for process-level crashes — and fallback chains should be cost-adjusted-quality ordered so worst-case spend during an outage stays bounded, which maps onto unimatrix's swarm.conf EXEC_CHAIN/VERIFY_MAP design already.
RECOMMENDATIONS:
- Formalize the tiering unimatrix is already researching: keep PLAN and final adjudication (ORCHESTRATOR/AUDITOR/REVIEWER roles) pinned to Fable 5 (the strongest available model) — this matches Anthropic's own lead-agent design and the Cursor economics data (planning is cheap in token volume, disproportionately valuable, and failure there propagates downstream).
- Do NOT let judge capability alone substitute for judge independence — 'smarter judge' is not a fix for self/family bias per 2026 research. Keep REVIEW/VERIFY lanes on a different model family than whichever lane produced the work being reviewed, exactly as swarm.conf's VERIFY_MAP + the codex↔kimi verifier-fallback pair already do; extend that same non-self-family constraint to any new adjudication role, not just first-pass review.
- When assigning Grok/GLM/haiku/sonnet as 'cheapest-capable executor,' bias toward giving them MORE detailed/explicit instructions from the planner rather than relying on their own judgment for ambiguous steps — Cursor's finding was that quality parity depended on the planner having already collapsed ambiguity; a cheap executor with a vague card is where quality gaps actually appear.
- Preserve unimatrix's context-centric (file-bus, full-artifact handoff) design over any temptation to move to role-based agent trees — this is validated directly by Anthropic's explicit warning against 'problem-centric decomposition' and by CrewAI/AutoGen's shared finding that handoff-state loss is the most common production failure across frameworks.
- Cap iteration/turn budgets per lane explicitly in swarm.conf/loop-discipline (mirrors the CrewAI max_iter lesson: unbounded loops are the #1 cost blowup vector) if not already fully enforced by MAX_ITERATIONS.
- For the fallback requirement (every role needs a qualified backup, including high-level roles): adopt an explicit ORDERED fallback chain per role (not just a single alternate), cost-adjusted so a cascading outage has a bounded worst-case spend — this is the pattern the resilience literature converges on and generalizes unimatrix's existing EXEC_CHAIN concept to REVIEW/PLAN roles too.
- Treat the generator-verifier asymmetry as time-limited, not permanent: as executor lanes (Grok/GLM) get more capable at coding-shaped tasks, budget for periodically re-checking whether the current verifier tier (Kimi/Codex) still has enough of a capability gap over the executors it grades — some 2026 sources report this gap narrowing specifically in coding domains.

## queue-reliability
Researched seven classic work-queue reliability patterns (SQS visibility timeout/lease, work stealing, DLQ, retry budgets, backoff+jitter, idempotency, poison-pill detection, health scoring) and mapped each to flag-file/JSONL/flock primitives usable on a local POSIX fs with no daemon. unimatrix already implements the lease+reclaim (LEASE_MIN heartbeat touch), a wall-clock watchdog (WORKER_TIMEOUT_SEC), a per-lane retry cap (MAX_LANE_RETRIES), and a TTL park file (.bus/limits/<lane>.limited) — these map directly to SQS visibility-timeout, hung-worker kill, bounded-retry, and circuit-breaker-open patterns respectively. What's missing for the proposed role-tiered universal-fallback future: (1) no dead-letter queue — MAX_LANE_RETRIES-exhausted jobs just fail the run, nothing parks them for later replay/inspection; (2) no jittered backoff between retries, only immediate same-lane or straight-to-fallback re-fire; (3) no idempotency key/fencing on job records, so a requeue-after-crash can double-execute a side-effecting (write-capable) worker; (4) no poison-pill quarantine distinct from rate-limit parking — a card that crashes every lane for content reasons (not quota reasons) is retried the same way as a transient failure, forever eligible; (5) no persistent per-lane health score — MAX_LANE_RETRIES and .limited are per-run/per-TTL, not a rolling reputation that would let planning skip a chronically-flaky lane before spending a turn on it; (6) no work-stealing/fallback at the ROLE level — VERIFY_MAP only reroutes verifier lanes, nothing reroutes if the fixed judge/planner/reviewer role itself is limited.
KEY POINTS:
- SQS pattern: visibility timeout hides a claimed message from other consumers for a bounded window; the worker must delete (ack) before it expires or the message reappears for redelivery — on a file-bus this is exactly LEASE_MIN + heartbeat touch on the claimed/<id>.<worker> file, reclaim keyed on heartbeat mtime age not claim-time age (unimatrix already does this per bus-discipline.md 'Leases' section). Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSSimpleQueueService/latest/SQSDeveloperGuide/sqs-visibility-timeout.html
- Dead-letter queue: after maxReceiveCount (commonly 3-5) delivery attempts, the message is moved out of the live queue into a DLQ for inspection/manual redrive, converting an unbounded contended retry loop into a bounded one and capping blast radius of one bad payload. File-bus equivalent: on MAX_LANE_RETRIES exhaustion, mv the job into .bus/dead/<id>.json (not just fail the run) carrying the full failure history (which lanes tried, error excerpts) so a human or a later swarm run can redrive it; unimatrix today has no .bus/dead/ — exhausted retries just fail the branch. Source: https://robinali34.github.io/blog_system_design/2025/11/13/dead-letter-queue-guide/
- Exponential backoff + jitter: retry delay doubles each attempt (1s,2s,4s,8s...) but full/decorrelated jitter randomizes it to avoid thundering-herd retry storms when many workers fail simultaneously (e.g. a shared rate limit trips). File-bus equivalent: before re-queueing a failed job (mv claimed/failed -> queue/), sleep a jittered $((base * 2**attempt + RANDOM % base)) computed from a per-job attempt counter stored in the JSONL record or filename suffix; unimatrix currently has no backoff at all between MAX_LANE_RETRIES attempts or before lane failover — retries and failovers fire immediately, which is the exact retry-storm shape this pattern exists to prevent, especially relevant when a 429/limited event on one lane correlates with several in-flight cards hitting it at once. Source: https://d1.awsstatic.com/builderslibrary/pdfs/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter.pdf
- Retry budget: Google SRE caps retries at 3 attempts per request AND a client-wide ceiling (~10% of total request volume may be retries) so retry traffic can't itself become the overload; once the ratio is exceeded, further retries are refused until it drops. unimatrix has a per-lane MAX_LANE_RETRIES (job-scoped) but no run-wide or lane-wide retry-volume ceiling — a run with many hard cards could still burn arbitrary total retry spend, which conflicts with the project's BUDGET_USD intent. A run-level retry-budget counter (e.g. .bus/limits/retry-budget.count vs a run-wide cap derived from FANOUT/job count) would close this. Source: https://sre.google/sre-book/handling-overload/
- Idempotency: at-least-once delivery means a consumer may see the same message 2-3 times after a crash/requeue; safety requires the consumer to be idempotent — typically via a unique idempotency key checked against a 'seen' store before acting, so re-delivery is a no-op rather than a double side effect. unimatrix's bus-discipline.md already states the principle ('a duplicate claim or replay of the same id is a no-op, not a double-execution — the id is what a reader checks') but this is aspirational for read-only lanes; for WRITE-capable lanes (FR-15 sidecar, grok/kimi/GLM write mode) a stale-lease reclaim that fires while the original worker is still alive-but-slow (heartbeat race) could let two processes both apply the same edit/commit. A cheap fix: before reclaim, the reaper writes a fenced 'stolen' marker the original worker must check (or checks a monotonic epoch/lease-generation number) before its own write lands — classic fencing-token pattern, currently absent. Source: https://microservices.io/patterns/communication-style/idempotent-consumer.html, https://www.morling.dev/blog/on-idempotency-keys/
- Poison pill: a message that fails deterministically regardless of retries (malformed payload, unrecoverable logic error) as opposed to a transient failure (timeout, rate limit) — detection is purely delivery-count-based (SQS/Azure Service Bus both just count attempts, they don't try to semantically distinguish transient vs permanent) and the fix is quarantine, not more retries. unimatrix's MAX_LANE_RETRIES + failover chain treats every failure the same way (retry, then next lane in EXEC_CHAIN) with no separate signal for 'this card itself is broken' vs 'this lane is broken' — a poison card burns through the ENTIRE exec chain on every lane before landing in the same undifferentiated failure state as a quota-limited card. A distinguishing flag (.bus/poison/<id> once a card has failed on >=2 semantically-different lanes with non-quota errors) would let the orchestrator stop retrying and escalate to Fable/human instead of re-spending across all remaining lanes. Source: https://www.task-queues.com/queue-fundamentals-architecture/dead-letter-queues-poison-messages/
- Work stealing: idle workers pull tasks from a busy peer's queue tail (not their own head) rather than sitting idle while another worker has a backlog; classically implemented with per-worker deques and a lightweight lock only on the steal path, so local push/pop stays lock-free. On a file-bus this is nearly free since queue/ is already a shared pool — FANOUT's job-pool already gives implicit stealing at the job level (any free pool slot picks the next queue/ file). What's NOT stolen today is ROLE-level: if REVIEW=codex:default is itself limited/hung, nothing steals the reviewer role onto another qualified lane — VERIFY_MAP only rotates verifier assignment for generation lanes, there's no equivalent 'if the fixed REVIEW role's lane is .limited, substitute the next role-qualified lane' fallback, which is exactly the gap the user's proposed tiered-role-with-universal-fallback design needs to close. Source: https://every-algorithm.github.io/2025/02/27/work_stealing.html, https://stack.convex.dev/work-stealing
- Health scoring: rather than a hard binary limited/not-limited flag, production load balancers track a rolling EWMA (exponential moving average, alpha~0.3) of per-worker success rate and latency, so a node with a bad recent streak gets deprioritized (lower routing weight) without being fully cut off, and recovers gradually as fresh successes accumulate — this is strictly more informative than unimatrix's current .bus/limits/<lane>.limited (binary, TTL-expiring, only set on explicit 429/quota signals). unimatrix has no persistent cross-run health signal at all: MAX_LANE_RETRIES and .limited both reset per run/TTL, so a lane that failed 4 of its last 5 cards on THIS run gets no different treatment on the NEXT swarm invocation. A simple file-based EWMA — one line per lane in .bus/health/<lane>.score, updated on every job outcome, read by swarm-run's lane-selection logic to skip/deprioritize a lane below a threshold before spending a turn on it — is the missing piece for 'health scoring of workers' specifically. Source: https://agentgateway.dev/docs/kubernetes/main/llm/load-balancing/
- Circuit breaker (adjacent to health scoring): trips open after a failure-rate/count threshold, stays open for a cooling-off period (refuses all calls, fails fast), then half-opens to test recovery with a trial call before fully closing again. unimatrix's .limits/<lane>.limited TTL park is functionally the open+cooling-off state; what's missing is the half-open probe — today a lane just becomes eligible again the instant the TTL file's age check passes, with no single canary job to confirm recovery before the full job stream resumes onto it, so a lane that's still down gets the next queued card thrown at it at full volume. Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/circuit-breaker
- Maildir/beanstalkd precedent for the whole no-daemon approach: djb's Maildir format (tmp/ -> new/ -> cur/ via atomic rename, msg-per-file) is the original proof that a mail-delivery-grade at-least-once queue needs zero daemon/DB, only POSIX rename() atomicity — validates unimatrix's own queue/claimed/done directory-rename design as a known-good pattern, not a hack. beanstalkd (a real daemon, but useful as a reference model) formalizes the job states unimatrix's directories informally encode: ready(queue/) -> reserved(claimed/, with TTR=LEASE_MIN) -> done, plus a 'buried' state (bury command, FIFO side-list, untouched until manually kicked) that is precisely the poison-pill quarantine unimatrix lacks — 'buried' is a state a human/orchestrator actively re-admits, not a TTL that silently expires. Sources: https://cr.yp.to/proto/maildir.html, https://github.com/beanstalkd/beanstalkd/blob/master/doc/protocol.txt
RECOMMENDATIONS:
- Add a .bus/dead/ directory: MAX_LANE_RETRIES exhaustion on a card moves it to .bus/dead/<id>.json (full attempt history: lanes tried, error excerpts, timestamps) instead of just failing the run — gives redrive-ability and matches DLQ practice.
- Add jittered backoff before same-lane retry and before lane failover: sleep base*2^attempt + random jitter, derived from an attempt counter already recoverable from the JSONL run log — cheap, prevents retry-storm correlation with a shared rate-limit trip across concurrent cards.
- Add a run-wide (or lane-wide) retry-budget ceiling, not just the existing per-job MAX_LANE_RETRIES, so a batch of hard cards can't collectively blow past BUDGET_USD purely on retry spend.
- Add a fencing/epoch check on stale-lease reclaim for WRITE-capable lanes specifically: bump a lease-generation number on reclaim, have the (possibly still-alive) original worker's write path check it before landing an edit, closing the double-execution race the current heartbeat-only reclaim allows.
- Add poison-pill quarantine distinct from rate-limit parking: a card that fails on >=2 lanes for non-quota reasons goes to .bus/poison/<id> and escalates to Fable/human rather than continuing to burn through EXEC_CHAIN — mirrors beanstalkd 'bury', which is manually re-admitted rather than TTL-expiring.
- Add a persistent per-lane health score (.bus/health/<lane>.score, simple EWMA on job outcomes, updated every job, read at lane-selection time) so chronic flakiness deprioritizes a lane across runs, not just within the .limited TTL window of a single run — this is the load-bearing piece for the proposed role-tiered universal-fallback design, since it lets planning route AROUND a historically bad lane before spending a turn, not just react after N in-run failures.
- Extend fallback beyond EXEC_CHAIN to the fixed ROLES themselves: today VERIFY_MAP reroutes verifier lanes but PLAN/ORCHESTRATOR/REVIEW have no fallback if their pinned lane is .limited — for the proposed tiered hierarchy (Kimi/Codex as high-level review/verify, Grok/GLM as bulk exec, claude tiers as cheapest-capable), every role needs a documented fallback lane, matching swarm.conf's own EXEC_CHAIN pattern but applied to REVIEW/verify-role assignment, not just generation.
- Add a half-open probe step when a .limited TTL expires: send one canary job before resuming full job flow onto a previously-limited lane, rather than immediately re-admitting it to the FANOUT pool at full volume — classic circuit-breaker half-open, currently missing.

## repo-fallback-state

Unimatrix has real, working failover machinery, but it is entirely LANE-level within a single EXEC_CHAIN role, plus one narrow LANE-pair fallback (codex<->kimi) scoped only to the Phase-E verify subcommand — there is no ROLE-level fallback concept anywhere in the codebase. PLAN and ORCHESTRATOR (both hardcoded to 'fable', the live session) have zero fallback by architecture — lane_cmd refuses to ever spawn 'fable'. REVIEW, used as swarm-loop's judge, is a hard single-lane pin with no chain and no pair fallback (VERIFY_MAP/pair-fallback only applies to swarm-run.sh's separate `verify` subcommand, not to swarm-loop's judge role) — worse, a limited judge lane isn't even parked, it's silently un-claimable, and the pool's gate can spin for the full 5h TTL with no watchdog catching it since WORKER_TIMEOUT_SEC only bounds an already-spawned worker. claude and gemini lanes have no limit_error() signature detection at all (absent from the case statement, falling into a bare catch-all), so a rate limit on either is invisible to the whole failover system. EXEC_CHAIN exhaustion correctly parks loudly (FR-10) but only escalates to a human, never to a higher config tier. Getting to "every role and every lane has an automatic qualified fallback" requires new role-scoped chain/pair config (a REVIEW_CHAIN or generalized VERIFY_MAP fallback list, a stall-timeout for pinned-but-never-claimed specs) plus closing the claude/gemini detection gap — and the PLAN/ORCHESTRATOR gap is architecturally blocked by the documented "no unattended driver in v1" Non-Goal, not just an oversight.

## pm-brainstorm
22 distinct feature ideas for unimatrix's proposed role-tiered + universal-fallback future, grounded in the actual repo (swarm.conf's EXEC_CHAIN/VERIFY_MAP/MAX_LANE_RETRIES, the speedwars ledger at spec 08, and the GLM/Kimi/Grok child-env spawn contracts in rules/unimatrix/model-lanes.md). The set spans every theme named in the brief — per-role fallback matrices, speedwars-driven and live-canary routing signals, lane health/reputation scoring, auth-freshness preflight, escalation and demotion ladders, N-lane review-pair generalization, Fable's own single-point-of-failure as orchestrator, PAYG-budget-aware kimi routing, real-header circuit breakers, work-stealing, and mixed-tier quorum verification — plus several not explicitly requested (capability tags, blast-radius-gated role escalation, incident postmortems, confidence self-reporting, graceful total-outage degrade, manual pin escape hatch, and a fallback row for the proposal's own new spec-critique role). A strong majority of the ideas are '0-2 line' v1s because unimatrix already has the scaffolding they'd reuse: an empty .bus/limits/ directory built for circuit-breaker state, a speedwars ledger already carrying cost/complexity/pinned fields, and a VERIFY_MAP string format that generalizes to N lanes by just adding commas. The two genuinely hard ones are Fable-self-fallback (the orchestrator role is architecturally 'never spawned', so no clean automated handoff exists yet) and true health-score auto-disable (needs a human-in-the-loop v1 before it's safe to automate).
KEY POINTS:
- Role Fallback Matrix (RFM) — a ROLE_FALLBACK_MAP config table (PLAN/ORCHESTRATOR/REVIEW/VERIFY/EXEC → ordered lane:model list), separate from EXEC_CHAIN, so 'who covers REVIEW when codex is down' has its own answer instead of falling through the exec chain. Cheapest v1: one bash assoc array in swarm.conf, same VERIFY_MAP string syntax already used, read wherever REVIEW=codex:default is currently hardcoded.
- Speedwars-Driven Auto-Chain — read docs/ops/speedwars.jsonl's $-per-verified-done and median wall_secs per lane×complexity bucket and reorder EXEC_CHAIN for that tier. Cheapest v1: one jq query appended to the existing speedwars-report.sh that PRINTS a suggested chain string for the operator to paste in — no auto-write, human stays in the loop.
- Lane Health Score — rolling 0-100 score per lane from recent speedwars rows (recency-weighted success rate, false-done rate, timeout rate); lanes below threshold get auto-skipped in fallback chains until they recover. Cheapest v1: a jq query over the last N rows surfaced in /speedwars output; skip stays a manual SKIP_LANE=grok toggle a human flips after eyeballing the score, not an auto-disable.
- Pre-Wave Canary Probe — send a trivial 'reply OK' prompt to each lane in the chain right before a real fanout and drop any lane that fails/hangs for THIS wave only (live signal, distinct from speedwars' historical signal). Cheapest v1: reuse the existing lane_cmd() spawn function with a fixed prompt and a short timeout override, run serially in swarm-run.sh before the real dispatch.
- Auth-Freshness Preflight — check each lane's credential isn't stale (grok OAuth file age, GLM/Kimi key present in env.master) before a wave, so a worker doesn't burn its whole WORKER_TIMEOUT_SEC discovering an expired token mid-run. Cheapest v1: a case statement per lane checking credential file mtime/presence at swarm-run.sh startup, warning-only (non-blocking) in v1.
- Escalation Ladder (haiku→sonnet→opus→kimi) — on verify failure, retry the SAME card at the next rung of a capability ladder before crossing providers, codified per-role. Cheapest v1: this needs zero new code — it's purely an EXEC_CHAIN ordering convention (claude:haiku claude:sonnet claude:opus kimi:kimi-k3) plus a doc note; MAX_LANE_RETRIES already walks a chain.
- Demotion Ladder (trivial cards downshift) — PLAN tags each card's complexity at seed time (reusing the existing speedwars C1-C5 rubric); C1/C2 cards get a per-card override to the cheapest lane (glm/haiku) even if EXEC_CHAIN's default order is pricier. Cheapest v1: one conditional at seed time — complexity<=C2 prepends glm:default to that card's lane override; the complexity field already gets written by run-meta rows (spec 08 FR-4), so this is reuse, not a new field.
- N-Lane Review Pair Rotation — generalize VERIFY_MAP from fixed pairs into a comma-separated pool per role (claude:codex,kimi) so review load and single-lane review bias spread across N lanes via round-robin by card id hash. Cheapest v1: a few lines in the existing verify-picker function to split on comma and rotate — no new data structure, VERIFY_MAP's syntax barely changes.
- Fable-Self-Fallback — define what happens when the live session (PLAN+ORCHESTRATOR, 'never spawned') hits a rate limit or context wall mid-run: pause, checkpoint (bus state is already durable on disk), and either wait-resume or hand gate-only duties to a backup lane with a stripped orchestrator prompt. Cheapest v1: a documented manual runbook, not automation — orchestrator is architecturally never-spawned today, so full automation is a bigger ask than the other 21 ideas here.
- Budget-Aware Kimi Router — kimi is real PAYG dollars unlike pooled glm/grok/codex; route to kimi only while BUDGET_USD headroom covers the card's estimated cost, falling through the chain once the run's running total crosses a threshold. Cheapest v1: sum cost_usd from speedwars rows written so far in-run via jq before each kimi spawn decision — one guard clause reusing an existing write path, no new counter infra.
- Circuit-Breaker TTL from Real Reset Headers — on a 429, parse the provider's actual rate-limit-reset value (already partially captured for failover sniffing) into a per-lane cooldown-until timestamp instead of a fixed guess. Cheapest v1: a flat file .bus/limits/<lane>.until with the epoch reset time, checked before spawn — .bus/limits/ already exists in the repo's bus tree per CLAUDE.md, so this fills existing scaffolding rather than adding new.
- Idle-Lane Work-Stealing — an idle lane atomically claims from another lane's backlog via the same file-bus claim mechanism already used generically, instead of sitting idle while one lane queues up. Cheapest v1: verify queue/ claims are already lane-agnostic; if so this is a config/labeling fix (drop any lane-affinity tag) rather than new code.
- Mixed-Tier Verify Panel (N-of-M quorum) — for C4/C5 cards, require 2-of-3 independent lanes to agree verified before accepting; disagreement escalates to Fable for adjudication. Cheapest v1: opt-in only (flagged at seed time), spawns two verifiers from idea 8's rotation pool and diffs their verdict field, escalating only on mismatch.
- Role Capability Tags — a LANE_CAPS assoc array (can-review, can-synthesize, can-critique-spec, write-capable) so role fallback matrices can be validated at config-load time and refuse a chain naming a lane that lacks a role's required tag. Cheapest v1: one static array plus a 5-line validator called once at startup.
- Reputation Decay — lane health score (see above) weights recent runs higher via exponential decay so a lane that had a bad week climbs back into rotation without a manual reset. Cheapest v1: change the health-score jq aggregation from a flat N-row average to age-weighted — a formula tweak, no new infrastructure.
- Dry-Run Cost/Time Estimator — before a wave commits, print estimated $ and wall-clock range per card from speedwars medians for that lane×complexity bucket. Cheapest v1: a `/swarm --estimate` flag that runs the seed step, prints the jq-derived table, and exits before any worker spawns.
- Blast-Radius-Gated Role Escalation — escalating a role (not just an exec card) up the ladder requires the new lane be pre-approved for that role's risk level (read-only review vs a merge-worthy adjudication verdict), reusing the existing write-capable/read-only distinction that already gates FR-15 sidecars. Cheapest v1: apply the existing write-capable flag to non-exec roles — no new permission model.
- Incident Postmortem Auto-Note — whenever a role/lane fallback actually fires, append one JSONL line (fallback_reason: 429/timeout/false-done) capturing the trigger, so patterns become visible without reconstructing from raw bus logs. Cheapest v1: piggyback on the existing speed_row() append call site (spec 08 FR-1) with one added field, rather than a new file.
- Confidence Self-Report Field — prompt each executor to end its answer with 'CONFIDENCE: N' (1-5); low self-reported confidence auto-routes to review even for cards that wouldn't otherwise trigger a verify wave. Cheapest v1: a regex grep on the handoff file for the CONFIDENCE line, advisory-only (logged, not gated) in v1 — purely additive to the existing prompt template.
- Graceful All-Lanes-Down Degrade Mode — when every lane in a role's fallback matrix is simultaneously benched (circuit-breaker + health score + auth-preflight all red), stop silently retrying and park the run with a clear 'blocked, human needed' state instead of looping to MAX_ITERATIONS. Cheapest v1: one check at the top of the claim loop — empty filtered chain writes .bus/loop/blocked and exits early; Ground Control cockpit already polls bus state so it surfaces this for free.
- Per-Card Lane Pinning Override — a manual escape hatch so a human can pin a stubborn card to a known-good lane, bypassing all the automatic routing above. Cheapest v1: 'pinned' already exists as a speedwars field (spec 08) — just expose it as a first-class `/swarm pin <id> <lane:model>` command instead of manual bus-file editing.
- Spec-Critique Role Fallback (Kimi/Codex pairing) — the proposed tiered future promotes Kimi+Codex to spec-critique/hard-synthesis duties; give that new role its own fallback-matrix row so a spec-critique pass doesn't silently skip when Kimi is budget-capped and Codex is mid-wave busy. Cheapest v1: one more entry in idea 1's ROLE_FALLBACK_MAP — SPEC_CRITIQUE="kimi:kimi-k3 codex:default gemini:default" — no new mechanism, just applying the existing table to the proposal's new role name.
RECOMMENDATIONS:
- Ship the near-zero-cost wins first: Role Fallback Matrix (idea 1), Circuit-Breaker TTL (idea 11, fills the already-empty .bus/limits/ scaffold), and the Escalation Ladder (idea 6, pure EXEC_CHAIN convention + doc note, no code) — all reuse existing structures and de-risk the rest.
